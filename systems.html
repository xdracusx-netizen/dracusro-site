Systems guide

The systems, in short.

The short version of every custom system here — enough to know what you are signing up for. The rest is for you to find in the world, which is rather the point.

01 · Citizenship

You pick a side, and it decides most of your game

Not a chat channel. Your faction has its own city to build, its own dungeons, its own world boss, its own gear to farm and its own cosmetics. It also decides who you are at war with on Saturday. Each faction holds one thing the other four need, so nobody can afford to cut anybody off.

Leaving is possible, and deliberately slow

You cannot switch until the city you belong to has reached City tier. Nobody gets to abandon a settlement the moment it looks like hard work.

After that, every move costs more than the last.

MoveCooldown before the next one
First switch1 month
Second switch3 months
Third switch9 months

Whatever you contributed stays with the city you contributed it to. This exists so a faction losing badly can bleed players — not so you can chase whoever is winning this week.

02 · Cities

Your city grows because people played in it

Every kill, every clear, every delivery pays into your city. Cities move through five tiers, and each tier is a physically different map — more buildings, more services, more of a place.

TierOpens
SettlementBasic shops. Tier 1 dungeon.
VillageExpanded gear, base refining, tier 2 dungeon.
TownCrafting and enhancing. Tier 3 dungeon.
CityTier 4 dungeon. World bosses activate.
MetropolisTier 5, legendary crafting, god items. Needs a conquest, not just development.
Access never revokes. Once a dungeon opens for your city it stays open. Fall behind and it costs a fee to keep using it — and that fee goes straight back into your city. Falling behind costs money, never content.
03 · Multiclassing

One character holds every class

You do not roll an alt. Unlock a class once and it stays unlocked — then walk into your faction city and change into it whenever you want. Same character, same name, same collection progress, different job.

  • Swapping happens at an NPC in your faction city, not mid-fight and not mid-siege.
  • Everything you have earned follows you into every class you hold.
  • Human classes only, all the way through 4th job. No Doram, no Baby classes.
What this changes. A party that needs a healer stops looking for one and just decides who becomes one. Grouping gets much easier. The flip side is that your class stops being your identity — nobody is the priest any more, because anyone can be. We think that trade is worth making.
04 · Bestiary & card album

Every monster is worth killing. Every card is worth something.

With no quest chains pointing you anywhere, these are what make one map worth choosing over another. The bestiary tracks kills per species and pays permanent bonuses at milestones. The album registers cards — once each, ever.

One registration per card is what creates the market. Over a thousand cards, all around 0.01%. Nobody finishes the album. So there is permanent demand for cards nobody wants to equip, and the junk other servers vendor has a buyer here.
05 · Dungeons & gear

Farm as long as you like. The ceiling is elsewhere.

Five tiers of instanced dungeon, solo or party. No daily lockout, no weekly lockout, no attunement. Party runs pay better drops and an experience bonus, and have mechanics the solo version does not.

Base gear drops freely and always will. The materials that upgrade the good stuff come from world bosses and from holding territory — things that happen on a schedule and cannot be ground out.

Materials are the ceiling, not hours played. What your faction won last week decides what you can wear this week. Refining uses protection ore only — you never destroy the item, you spend the ore.
06 · World bosses

The one time nobody is at war

Strictly PvE. No faction contest, no PvP flag, no fighting each other at the spawn. Everything else here is built to generate friction between factions. This is the release valve.

Contribution is not just damage — healing, damage taken while holding the boss's attention, and buff uptime all count, so the one fight where tanks and healers matter most actually pays them.

Contribution pools across your party. Twenty people pooling beats five people pooling. Bringing more players grows the reward instead of splitting it, so there is never a reason to keep the fight small.
07 · Caravans

Grinding builds cities. Trade funds armies.

Castle-holding guilds buy cargo in one city and move it to another. That cargo costs real money, and delivering it is where almost all siege supply comes from. A guild that never runs a convoy shows up on Saturday with nothing to spend.

  • Routes are instanced and the convoy is visible to the whole server. Raiders opt in deliberately — nobody gets dragged into PvP for standing on a road.
  • Longer hauls pay disproportionately more, and running the same route repeatedly pays less each time.
  • Anyone can raid. No castle, no guild, no siege obligation. This is the low-stakes way into PvP here.
Raiding destroys value on purpose. The owner loses everything, the raider recovers a fraction. Two factions farming each other burn more than they make — so banditry works against a real enemy and fails as a mutual arrangement.
08 · Sieges

Twenty against twenty, across three maps

Twenty-five castles. Three in each faction's main city, two in its secondary territory. Guilds hold them, citizens vote on whether they deserve to, and everything changes hands by force on a schedule everybody knows.

20 vs 20 25 castles scheduled windows three stages
1

The wall

Three control points. Breach the gate or scale the walls. Holding two of three drains the defender, so lost ground costs them whether or not anyone dies.

2

The courtyard

Open fighting inside the walls, with the points placed so defenders cannot just camp the throne door. Allied guilds who won early can reinforce from here.

3

The throne room

Break the emperium and the castle changes hands. Run out of supplies and it does not.

The emperium takes area damage. Everywhere else you defend it by standing on top of it in a pile. Here a defensive ball eats the same attacks it is trying to block, so you have to fight outward and hold the room instead.

Before the fighting, both sides spend from the guild's war chest on siege hardware. Whatever you do not spend becomes your respawn tickets — every ram is a life you do not have, and you buy blind. The war chest does not refill weekly, either: what you spent last week is still missing this week.

Everything else is for you to find. There is no wiki page that tells you where the good hunting is.
