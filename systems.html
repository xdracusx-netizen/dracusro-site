Systems guide

The systems, in short.

The short version of every custom system here — enough to know what you are signing up for. The rest is for you to find in the world, which is rather the point.

01 · Citizenship

You pick a side, and it decides most of your game

Not a chat channel. Your faction has its own city to build, its own dungeons, its own gear to farm and its own cosmetics. It also decides who you are at war with on Saturday.

Each faction holds one thing the other four need, and that is enforced rather than encouraged. Crush a faction and you lose whatever they were supplying — including services you were relying on.

FactionHoldsWhich means
PronteraGovernanceCollects double the tax it sets. Hardest walls on the server, and declares war at a lower threshold than anybody else.
MorrocSubterfugeBetter returns on raided caravans. Runs the black market, the only place stolen cargo can be sold.
GeffenResearchThree extra ranks on every shared research node, and converts one faction's crafting materials into another's.
PayonProductionA 44% material discount at their forge — available to anybody who travels there and pays the tax. They also sell the server's food and potions.
AlbertaTradePays nobody's tax, anywhere. Their auction house shows every listing on the server in one place.

National perks work from your first day

You do not research your way into them. A Payon citizen crafts cheaper the moment they log in, and research stacks on top of that.

Leaving is possible, and deliberately slow

Your city tier gates it, and every move costs more than the last.

MoveCooldown before the next one
First switch1 month
Second switch3 months
Third switch9 months

Whatever you contributed stays with the city you contributed it to.

02 · Cities

Your city grows because people played in it

Every kill, every clear, every delivery pays into your city. Cities move through five tiers, and each tier is a physically different map — more buildings, more services, more of a place.

TierOpens
SettlementBasic shops and crafting. Tier 1 dungeon. Food levels 1 and 2.
VillageTier 2 dungeon. Better recipes. Food levels 3 to 5.
TownTier 3 dungeon. Food levels 6 to 8. Permanent floor — you cannot fall below it.
CityTier 4 dungeon. Research unlocks. Your world boss starts spawning. Almighty and the top potions.
MetropolisTier 5 access, the highest food tier, and convoys that can carry tier 5 cargo. Needs a conquest, not just development.

Access never revokes. Once a dungeon opens for your city it stays open. Fall behind and it costs a fee to keep using it — falling behind costs money, never content.

City and Metropolis need maintaining

Both have a daily civic requirement. Fall short for seven days running and the tier drops. An active faction never notices; a faction that has stopped playing loses Metropolis in a week.

03 · Multiclassing

One character holds every class

You do not roll an alt. Unlock a class once and it stays unlocked — then walk into your faction city and change into it whenever you want. Same character, same name, same collection progress, different job.

Nothing has been added. There are no custom classes. Some have been reworked and the expanded ones — Ninja, Gunslinger, Taekwon, Star Gladiator, Soul Linker, Super Novice — have been removed.

Mastering a class pays forever

Reach maximum job level in a class and one stat rises permanently, for that character, in every class they play.

Job tier masteredBonus
First job+2
Second job+4
Third job+6
Fourth job+8

Twelve job lines across six stats. Sixteen per stat at full mastery, ninety-six in total — and it applies in PvP.

Your gear comes with you

Reforging converts an item into a different template within the same tier, keeping its refine level, its modifiers and its set membership. Thirty-two saved loadouts per character, applied in a single action.

Each class has a fixed loadout shape. Monks and Assassins never carry a shield; Crusaders always do. Without that, reforging would let anybody bolt a shield onto anything and class identity would last a fortnight.

04 · Classes & combat

If you have played this game before, read this

Nothing here is a nerf for its own sake. Every change targets a pattern that makes fights worse for everybody involved.

On this server
BuffsThirty minutes, party-wide, and they survive death. Rebuffing after every death was never the interesting part.
Asura StrikeCapped as a share of the target's maximum health, and the cap depends on what you hit — 70% in the open world, including MVPs and minibosses. 35% in PvP. 5% against scripted encounters — dungeon bosses and world bosses. In exchange it no longer empties you: the SP cost is a fraction of what it was, so casting it does not take you out of the fight. Monks stay a menace to anything roaming a field map.
Shattered KiAsura now marks the target for 20 seconds. Everybody hitting that target — the whole party, the whole raid — deals 25% more damage. Against a boss that is worth far more than any burst a monk could contribute alone.
Crowd controlDiminishing returns. Four applications and the target is immune for twenty seconds. Freeze, stun, sleep and stone share one counter.
Bard and DancerScreen-wide auras that follow the performer. One is enough; a second adds nothing.
ResurrectionOne rank, full health, two minute cooldown, per caster.
After-cast delayCut hard. Cast time is counterplay and stays — dead time is not and goes.
Level cap275 base, 60 job.

Full detail on the classes and combat wiki page.

05 · Bestiary & album

You have it from your first login

No quest, no unlock, no NPC. Your character wakes up with it. Every kill is recorded, every milestone pays, and it follows you into every class you play.

Detail
Bestiary1,142 species, seven milestones each. Experience for the first five, permanent stat and health bonuses for the last two.
Card album1,023 cards. Registering one grants a permanent bonus — and you can take it back out whenever you like.
Bound toThe character, not the account. With multiclassing, your main is your alt.

Nobody will finish either. Full completion is about 10.2 million kills. The server is balanced around a quarter of that.

Registering a card does not consume it. Which is what keeps the card market alive — putting a valuable card in the album is a decision, not a sacrifice.

06 · Dungeons

Twenty-one instances, no lockouts anywhere

Four dungeons per faction across four tiers, plus Glast Heim shared between everybody. No keys, no attunement, no daily reset.

TierOpens atGroupBoss
1Settlement1 to 5One mechanic
2Village1 to 5Two mechanics
3Town1 to 5Three mechanics
4City5Three plus a puzzle
5Metropolis + conquest20Five wings and a throne room

No queue. Every dungeon sits where it has always sat, with a guard at the entrance who takes the fee and opens the warp. A queue makes a dungeon a button.

No boss mods. Bosses telegraph with emotes one to two seconds before they act, consistently across all twenty-six encounters. A fight that needs an addon to read is a fight we failed to communicate.

Wipe and you resume from the chamber you reached. Tier 5 instances persist for seven days, so a raid can come back across several evenings.

07 · Crafting

Nothing is rolled. You choose.

No RNG anywhere in the chain. You select your modifiers, materials always drop, and every item can be reforged into something else.

Detail
ModifiersSelected, never rolled. A tier 5 piece carries five, and you decide which effect sits on which rung.
Materials100% drop rate. Farming costs time, never luck.
Card slotsEvery crafted weapon has four. Every crafted armour has one.
Set bonusesAt two, four and five pieces. They survive reforging, which is what makes multiclassing affordable.
AccessoriesA separate set from world bosses, carrying utility rather than damage — movement speed, cooldown reduction, cast delay.

You need every faction's dungeon. Crafting a tier 4 piece takes materials from all five factions' tier 4 dungeons. Nobody gears up inside their own borders, which is why the auction house matters.

Crafted gear is soulbound. Materials are not. The restriction exists so nobody can buy their way past the crafting system — not to shut down trade.

08 · World bosses

Nobody owns these, and nobody fights over them

Six server-wide PvE events. Everybody turns up and kills them together, including people who fought each other on Saturday. PvP is disabled on these maps entirely.

Detail
Five faction bossesSpawn every 24 hours. A faction reaching City tier is what puts theirs on the schedule — it does not make the boss theirs.
The sixthSomething else spawns when all five faction bosses have died inside the same week. Weekly cadence. We are not saying what it is.
LootBy contribution, to everybody who fought. Nothing to tag, nothing to snipe.
AccessoriesNeed materials from all five faction bosses. There is a better version, and it needs something the five do not drop.

Which creates a dependency nobody can solve alone. If a faction falls below City tier their boss stops spawning, and nobody on the server can finish an accessory — including whoever kept them down there.

Built for three organised guilds with the rest of the server assisting. The sixth encounter needs seven tanks, which is not a number one guild fields.

09 · Caravans

Where the zeny comes from

Your city pays for a convoy out of its own contribution. You escort it and get paid personally — or you rob somebody else's and get paid personally.

WhoPaysEarns
The factionCivic contribution from the treasuryMilitary points, which fund wars
The escortNothing but time and riskZeny, plus military for the faction
The raiderNothing at allZeny

Escorting is the best zeny per hour in the game, at any level. A party of two at level 90 earns roughly what a fully geared endgame farmer makes grinding. It is how a player who is not yet strong enough to farm efficiently makes real money.

You cannot raid your own faction. Raiders lose nothing when they fail — no zeny, no points, no equipment, only time.

A convoy that arrives costs the city almost nothing. One that is lost hands most of its value to the faction that took it.

10 · Sieges

Saturday afternoon, all of it at once

Three stages on separate maps, a supply economy behind them, and territory that stays taken. This is not War of Emperium.

Detail
WhenSaturday afternoon, Pacific. Two hours — standard wars first, rebellions and civil wars second.
SizeTwenty against twenty, per castle
StagesThe wall, the courtyard, the throne room
SupplyMilitary points, spent on emplacements, siege hardware and respawn tickets. Purchases are blind.

A faction attacks with all five guilds or it does not attack. One declaration commits everybody, and it needs all five guild leaders to agree. Five guilds who cannot get along do not go to war.

Every siege on the server runs at the same time, so a guild can only be in one place. Every declaration is also a decision about what you are leaving undefended.

The supply pool does not refill. A faction that fights a brutal defence is genuinely poor afterwards — which is why the faction quietly trading while everybody else fought takes a castle next week.

Registration is open

The world has no owners. That is the interesting part.

Twenty-five castles, all unclaimed. Five cities, all still settlements. Nothing on this server has happened yet, and when it starts, it starts for everybody at once.
