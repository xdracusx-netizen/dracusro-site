How development works

Every monster killed, every dungeon cleared, every caravan delivered pays civic contribution into your city. When the total crosses a threshold, the city advances a tier and the map is replaced with a larger one.

Contribution is measured per citizen, not in raw total. A faction with three hundred people does not automatically outgrow one with fifty — it has to keep three hundred people playing. A large, idle population develops more slowly than a small, active one.

Why per-capita. If growth were raw total, the largest faction would win permanently and everyone would stampede into it. Measuring per active citizen means joining the biggest faction makes it grow more slowly, which is the brake on that.

The five tiers

SettlementTents and a few buildings. No wall. People arrived recently and have not finished.Basic shops. Tier 1 dungeon.
VillageA wall appears. First amenities — an inn, a tavern.Expanded gear, base refining, tier 2 dungeon.
TownIndustry starts. Makeshift premises for each trade. A church going up.Crafting and enhancing. Tier 3 dungeon.
CityThe city you would recognise.Tier 4 dungeon. World boss activates. Expanded recipes.
MetropolisDistricts, wealth, excess. A wealthy quarter, a presentable one, and an underbelly.Tier 5 access, legendary crafting, god items.

The wall appearing at Village is the clearest visual marker. A settlement is open ground; a village is defended. You can tell what tier a city is at a glance.

Metropolis is different

Every other tier is reached by development alone. Metropolis additionally requires a conquest — the faction must be holding territory it took from someone else. You cannot grind your way to the top tier while staying out of the war.

Thresholds

Each tier needs a cumulative contribution total. The gaps widen sharply, so Settlement to Village happens in days and City to Metropolis is a project.

Not final

Threshold numbers are set but not published, because they are tuned against measured play rates rather than guessed. They will be listed here once the first season has produced real data.

Cities do not collapse

Once a city reaches Town, it cannot fall below it. That is a permanent floor.

Above Town, a city can lose tiers if contribution dries up — but the floor means a faction that has a bad month does not get sent back to tents. Metropolis is exempt from the floor, because it is conquest-gated and losing the conquest should mean losing the tier.

Access never revokes

This is the rule that matters most, and it is deliberate.

Once a dungeon opens for your city, it never closes again. If your city drops below the tier that unlocked it, you keep access — you just pay a fee to use it.

The fee is zeny plus civic contribution. The zeny is destroyed. The contribution goes straight back into your own city's development, which means the act of paying to stay in content also helps the city climb back up.

Why it works this way. A hundred casual players should never lose access to their content because ten organised ones lost a fight on Saturday. Punish the wallet, not the schedule.

Using another faction's content

The same fee applies if you want to use a dungeon belonging to a faction that is not yours. Their city receives the contribution. You get the dungeon, they get paid, and now you have a concrete reason to resent them.

Checking your city

In game, @contrib shows your faction's civic total and your own character's contribution. @factioninfo <id> shows standings for any faction, so you can see exactly how far ahead or behind you are.
