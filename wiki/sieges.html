The war

Caravans Sieges

The shape of it

Twenty-five castles exist. Three sit in each faction's main city and two in its secondary territory. Guilds hold them, citizens vote on whether they deserve to, and everything changes hands by force on a schedule everybody knows.

Guild size20
Engagement20 vs 20
Castles25 — five per faction
ScheduleFixed windows, announced in advance
StagesThree, fought in sequence on separate maps

Before anyone fights

Every siege starts with a spending decision, and it is the most important one you will make that night.

Both sides commit from the guild's war chest. That commitment splits between siege hardware and respawn tickets — whatever you do not spend on equipment becomes the lives you have.

Every ram is a life you do not have. Every ladder is a body that will not come back.

Purchases are simultaneous and blind. You do not see what the other side bought until you are already fighting, which makes it a read on your opponent rather than a solved shopping list. Buy all rams against a reinforced gate and you have burned supply for nothing. Buy ladders against a defender who massed on the gate and you walk in.

The catalogue depends on the city

What you can buy is set by the development tier of the castle's city, not by your guild. A Village-tier city fields basic rams. A Metropolis fields artillery and reinforced gates. Which means the people grinding to develop the city are directly arming the guilds that defend it.

The war chest does not refill

It is a running balance, not a weekly allowance. What you spent last week is still missing this week. Grind through a brutal defence and you are genuinely poor afterwards — which means the guild that has been quietly banking is the one that takes your castle next cycle.

Where does supply come from? Almost entirely from trade.

The three stages

1

The wall

Three control points — the gate at the centre and a turret at each corner. Attackers get in by breaching the gate with a ram or scaling the walls with ladders. Holding two of the three starts draining the defender's supplies, so ground lost costs them whether or not anyone dies. Retake a point and the drain stops, which is the window a counterattack lives in.

2

The courtyard

Open fighting inside the walls. Three more control points, two of them positioned nearer the attacker's approach so defenders have to push out rather than camp the throne door. Tolerance here is zero — the attacker holding any single point starts the drain. Allied guilds who finished their own siege early can send reinforcements from this stage onward.

3

The throne room

One objective. Break the emperium and the castle changes hands immediately. Run the attacker out of supplies and it does not.

The drain only ever runs one way

It always runs against the defender. Attackers spend supplies on respawns and hardware and are on a clock, but holding ground never costs them anything. That is what forces defenders to contest points instead of massing on one and waiting.

The emperium

On most servers, defending the emperium means standing on top of it in a pile and letting bodies absorb everything. That does not work here.

The emperium takes area damage. A defensive ball stacked on it eats the same area attacks it is trying to block. Clumping stops being a strategy, and defenders have to fight outward and hold the room instead.

Defender respawn is inside the throne room, with brief invulnerability and a radius attackers cannot enter. Spawn camping is prevented by the shape of the room rather than by a rule somebody has to enforce.

There are multiple entrances, placed so the defender cannot cover them all from the emperium itself.

Holding a castle

  • Castles start unclaimed and MVP-guarded. First claim is by emperium break, same as everyone else.
  • All citizens vote, not just guilds. A guild that holds a castle badly can be voted out by the people living under it.
  • A deposed guild gets the right to take it back. They also get trade rights while they do — contraband runs, hunted by their own faction, because their own faction is currently occupied.
  • Holding more castles makes you poorer at each one. Every castle past your home cuts how efficiently you convert income. Empires are expensive on purpose.

After it ends

Attacker winsThe castle changes hands. A grace period stops it being immediately re-taken. The evicted guild gets first refusal on liberating it. Tribute starts flowing to the conquering faction straight away.
Defender winsNothing changes hands. The attacker loses everything they spent. There is no consolation payout — a siege never generates war supply, it only consumes it.
