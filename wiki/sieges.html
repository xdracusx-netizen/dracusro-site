The war

Caravans Sieges

Three stages, a supply economy, and territory that stays taken. This is not War of Emperium.

When

Detail
DaySaturday
TimeEarly afternoon, Pacific
FrequencyWeekly
LengthTwo hours, in two blocks
HourWhat is fought
FirstStandard wars. One faction attacking another.
SecondRebellions and civil wars. Occupied factions liberating their castles, and landless guilds challenging their own.

Every siege on the server happens at once. Declaring during the week queues you for the next window.

All five guilds, or nobody

A faction attacks with everything or it does not attack. A declaration commits all five guilds against all five of the target's castles. There is no partial commitment and no guild held in reserve.

Which means a faction is either attacking or defending in a given hour, never both. And five guilds who cannot agree do not go to war — not because a rule forbids it, but because a war needs all five of them pointed at the same castle on the same afternoon.

Everything else in this design — approval ratings, civil war, research funding, the shared military pool — exists to make that agreement something guilds have to work at.

What that looks like

Say Prontera has subjugated Geffen, and Morroc has declared on Prontera.

HourPronteraMorrocGeffen
FirstDefending their own five castlesAttacking with all five guildsNot involved
SecondDefending the five Geffen castles they holdNot involvedRebelling with all five guilds

Prontera fights twice with the same twenty people per castle, back to back, out of one supply pool. A faction that has conquered and is also being invaded is in the worst position in the game, and it got there by winning.

Supply

Both sides commit military points from the faction pool before the first stage opens. That commitment splits between equipment and respawn tickets — whatever you do not spend on hardware becomes lives.

Purchases are blind. Neither side sees what the other bought until they are already fighting.

The pool does not refill. Points spent are gone. A faction that fights a brutal defence is genuinely poor afterwards, which is why the faction that quietly traded while everybody else fought takes a castle next week.

An attacker who breaches cheaply arrives at the throne room rich. A defender is fighting three separate holding actions and starts each with the same number of bodies.

A faction that could not afford the full pool feels it immediately. Three hundred tickets is a hundred per stage across twenty players — five deaths each before the stage is lost.

The drain

Defenders lose tickets to deaths and to conceding ground. Attackers only lose them to deaths.

Attacker controlTickets drained per minute
Below the stage thresholdNone
One point above9
Two points18
Three points27

A defender holding nothing loses their stage allocation in under six minutes. Retaking a single point cuts the bleeding by a third immediately, which is the window a counterattack lives in.

So a defence that turtles the throne door does not survive — it haemorrhages. The drain is what forces defenders out into open ground.

The courtyard's point placement forces defenders out. Two of the three sit closer to where the attackers came in, so camping the throne door concedes the drain immediately.

Emplacements

The slots are fixed. What goes in them is not. Every castle has the same positions; you decide what each one holds, fresh before every siege.

EmplacementEffect
Arrow towerSustained damage in range
Slowing towerReduced movement speed
Sundering towerReduced armour and damage reduction
Dampening towerReduced damage dealt
Blight towerReduced healing received
BarricadeBlocks one approach until destroyed
Reinforced gateSubstantially more damage to breach

None of these kill anybody. They make attackers slower, softer, weaker and harder to heal. A tower that killed people would just be a twenty-first defender.

Four slots is enough for two pairings, which is the decision. A defender covering every angle weakly is easier to break than one covering two angles properly.

Artillery outranges every tower by double, which makes it the only thing that dismantles a fortified wall without anybody walking into it. It is also fragile enough that a defender sortieing out of the gate to kill it is a real play.

Morroc's hook is the one that changes defensive behaviour most. The counter is watching the whole wall rather than massing on the gate, which is exactly what the control point layout is already trying to force.

The emperium

It takes area damage. Standing on it in a pile means your own bodies absorb everything aimed at it. Clumping is actively harmful.
  • Devotion cannot target it. Nor can any damage-redirection effect.
  • Defenders respawn inside the room, with brief invulnerability and a radius attackers cannot enter.
  • Multiple entrances, placed so no single position covers them all.

How a siege ends

ConditionResult
The emperium is brokenThe castle changes hands. This is the only way a castle is taken.
Attacker pool reaches zeroAttackers are routed. The castle holds.
Defender pool reaches zeroDefenders cannot respawn. The attackers walk in and break it unopposed.
One hour elapsesThe defenders win.

An attacker who spent days of trading and could not break through in an hour has failed. The defender held, which was the only thing they were asked to do.

Reinforcement

A guild whose own siege resolves early may reinforce another still fighting, from any stage.

  • Reinforcements fill the existing twenty slots as replacements. A siege never becomes forty against twenty.
  • They can take over an assault using their own pool if the original guild runs dry.
  • Attackers are only routed when every attacking guild present has exhausted its pool.

Which rewards winning fast rather than just winning. A guild that grinds out a victory at fifty-eight minutes helped nobody. One that clears in twenty has forty minutes to spend somewhere it matters.

Holding what you took

Weeks heldIncome efficiency
On capture50%
After one week75%
After two weeks100%
  • No grace period. A castle can be attacked again the following week by anybody who can fund it.
  • The evicted guild gets first refusal on liberating it, in the second hour.
  • Approval is voted weekly by all citizens. Below 70% and a landless guild can challenge you.

Holding ten castles means ten defences across two hours, out of one pool. A faction that has not out-traded its own ambition ends up defending everything thinly, and loses castles every week until it is back to what it can afford.
