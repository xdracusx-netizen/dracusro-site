The war

Caravans Sieges

What each class is for, how it wins, and what kills it. Third job is the ceiling on this server, so these twelve are the final forms.

This is a preview and all of it is subject to change. These are design targets, not shipped values. Numbers move during testing and some of these classes will play differently by launch. What should not change is the identity — what each class is for.

The rule behind all of it

Every class has something it does for the group that nobody else does. That is deliberate, and it is the thing being protected when numbers get tuned.

Rules differ by class on purpose. Each one is balanced on its own terms. They are not meant to play the same, and a change that makes sense for one is often wrong for another.

Frontline

Rune Knight — the bruiser

Two weapon identities, and they play differently. Spear is the diver: go in, hit the main target, weave debuffs and Dragon Breath. Sword is the auto-attacker: Giant Growth, Enchant Blade and Two-Hand Quicken drive the damage, with area skills on cooldown.

Detail
Wins byGetting on top of somebody and staying there
Group hookHolds the middle and protects flanks so squishier classes can work
Dies toKiting, and anything that holds it still away from the fight
NotableDragon Breath is rebuilt on attack power — no more health scaling. Death Bound becomes a real counter-window rather than a reflect.

The Rune Knight tree drops most of its old Knight and Lord Knight damage skills. A character still levelling Knight keeps everything; the trimming applies once they graduate.

Royal Guard — the wall

Chooses who cannot die, and pays for it. Devotion works out the damage against the protected ally's defence and hands it straight to the Royal Guard, skipping their own armour, Auto Guard and Reflect Shield. Protecting a squishy means eating squishy-sized hits.

Detail
Wins byKeeping somebody else alive through something that should have killed them
Group hookPicks who is untouchable, and holds the rhythm of a push
Dies toBeing stripped. Every shield buff needs the shield equipped.
NotableBanding caps at five, so it rewards a squad rather than a wall of twenty guards.

Royal Guard sits outside the damage bands on purpose, which is exactly why strip, dispel, Dark Claw and Poison Smoke exist. Every one of them is an answer to this class.

Genetic — the duelist

The crafter, and a sustained frontliner. Picks a target and dismantles it with plants, debuffs and acid. Hell's Plant on this server is an aura around the Genetic, not something parked in a doorway — it is a push tool.

Detail
Wins byMarking one target and grinding it down
Group hookCorroded — the mark that makes everything the Genetic brings hit harder
Dies toBeing forced off its target, or killed before the setup lands
NotableAcid Demonstration is capped on the target's health and no longer scales off their stats.

Its old win buttons all had no cooldown — Cart Cannon, Mandragora, a plant in a choke point, uncapped acid. All four now have windows.

Strikers

Sura — the initiator

Bruce Lee burst. Marks a target with Asura, combos in, finishes, and resets out with Body Relocation and Lightning Walk. Two routes: delete one target, or chunk a clump with Tiger Cannon.

Detail
Wins byLanding a finisher inside its own 20 second window
Group hookShattered Ki — everybody hitting the marked target does 25% more damage
Dies toAnything that survives the window. No protection on a ranged build.
NotableAsura is rebuilt on attack power and no longer drains all your SP. It is the setup, not the kill.

Against a marked target, Tiger Cannon and Gate of Hell hit for double. The mark cannot be dispelled by anything, and no second Asura can land while it holds.

Guillotine Cross — the executioner

One squishy target, from nowhere, then out before being pinned. Area and counter builds are kept for dungeons, but in PvP this class exists to remove a specific person.

Detail
Wins byReaching the person the group thought was safe
Group hookTakes the healer or the caster nobody else can get to
Dies toAnything that holds it still. It has no answer to being caught.
NotableKatar or dual daggers, never a shield. Dark Claw opens a damage window and stops the target reflecting melee.

The poison line is the anti-tank toolkit — Leech End ignores defence and reflect entirely, and Death Hurt is the natural counter to a healer who will not die.

Shadow Chaser — the saboteur

Stripper, copycat and trapper. The strip is the hardest Royal Guard answer in the game and stays strong — but it buys a kill window rather than a shutdown.

Detail
Wins byRemoving what the enemy was relying on at the moment they need it
Group hookTurns off the thing your team cannot fight through
Dies toBeing ignored — it contributes little damage of its own
NotableStrip runs on its own diminishing counter so Rogues keep their edge. Reproduce copies enemy skills, which keep our cooldowns automatically.

Strips no longer last minutes. They are a five second lockout on a cooldown — long enough to kill through, not long enough to remove somebody from the fight.

Range

Warlock — the artillery

Pokes with cast-time spells, commits big area damage at the right moment, and pops Telekinesis Intense to go all in. Keeps every Mage and Wizard spell.

Detail
Wins byLanding one enormous area skill on a group that clumped
Group hookNobody else clears a stack
Dies toAnything that reaches it. Highest ceiling, thinnest margin.
NotableSingle-target spells keep their stock cooldowns — the cast time is the limiter. Area skills get long separate cooldowns and hit proportionally harder.

Comet sits at 45 seconds. Gravitation Field at 30. These are decisions you make once per fight, not buttons you hold.

Ranger — the skirmisher

Ranged pressure with traps used in combat, not as area denial. Hunter and Sniper skills are all kept.

Detail
Wins byNever being where the fight is, and never letting up
Group hookTraps shape where the enemy is able to go
Dies toBeing closed on. Steady damage, never decisive alone.
NotableThree traps at a time — placing a fourth destroys the oldest. Friendly fire on traps stays on in guild versus guild.

Mechanic — the engineer

Two builds: Mado Gear artillery and axe brawler. A prepared position with turrets has to pose a real threat, because setting one up costs time.

Detail
Wins byHolding a position it had time to prepare
Group hookIts deployments become terrain the enemy has to deal with
Dies toBeing caught before setup. Ambushed, it is a person with an axe.
NotableTwo Silver Snipers and two Magic Decoys, and they fire at whatever the Mechanic is attacking. Self-Destruction is rebuilt on attack power.

Mechanic structures are the one exception to the summon budget — together they reach 80 to 100% of the Mechanic's own output, because they are stationary and the class is built around them.

Support

Arch Bishop — the pillar

A healing machine, not a deleter. Spot heals are spammable and limited by SP; percentage heals sit on long cooldowns for the moments that matter.

Detail
Wins byMaking somebody impossible to kill for long enough
Group hookEverything. The party functions because they are there.
Dies toBeing focused. Zero pressure — they cannot threaten anybody.
NotableHeal priest and battle priest are separate specs. An Arch Bishop cannot learn everything.

Healers are meant to be the prime target. Two Arch Bishops out-heal a single big hit in about a second, so killing through them takes two or three finishers landed inside a one to two second window. That is the intended shape of a fight.

Adoramus and Magnus Exorcismus are deliberately held back so a battle priest never replaces a Warlock.

Sorcerer — the controller

Shapes where fighting is possible. Control and area damage are one build; Spell Fist and the elementalist are the real forks. The kit stays multi-dimensional on purpose.

Detail
Wins byDeciding where the enemy is allowed to stand
Group hookDenies ground, and Dispel is the answer to buffs nobody else can remove
Dies toBeing rushed. Little damage, no healing.
NotableLand Protector is a 20 second window, not a permanent zone. Warmer is a push tool, not a camp.

Elemental spirits assist the Sorcerer's target rather than acting on their own. One at a time.

Minstrel and Wanderer — the performer

Reads the fight and picks the right song or dance; gets it wrong and dies. Screen-wide buffs and debuffs make the performer the first target on the field.

Detail
Wins byPicking correctly, over and over, while being hunted
Group hookOne performer, whole raid. A second adds nothing.
Dies toBeing found. Everything they provide ends when they do.
NotableGender-locked — male becomes Minstrel, female becomes Wanderer. About 90% of the kit is shared between them.
RuleDetail
AreaScreen-wide auras centred on the performer
LifetimeUp to 30 minutes, but only while the performer is alive
Performer diesEvery buff and debuff they provide ends immediately
StackingOne song and one dance per target, from any source
ChorusCounts two performers at most

Both sexes can fill both slots, so the one-song-one-dance rule works either way round.

What this means for a group

Stacking a class is fine if you spread it, wasteful if you concentrate it.

  • Marks never stack. Two Suras on one target is one wasted Sura. On two objectives, both are working.
  • Performers do not stack. One is the full effect.
  • Area effects thin out when they overlap. Sixteen plants in one doorway are not sixteen plants.
  • Burst overkills. Two executioners on the same target means the second one hit a corpse.

Which means a guild with six Suras is not wrong — they are a guild that has to attack six places at once. That is a strategy, and multiclassing means nobody is stuck with a composition that did not work.
