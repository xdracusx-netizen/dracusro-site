The war

Caravans Sieges

What each class is for, and exactly what changed from stock Ragnarok to get it there.

This is a preview and all of it is subject to change. These are design targets, not shipped values — numbers move during testing. What should not change is the identity: what each class is for.

How to read the tables

Stock is what the skill does in standard Ragnarok. Here is what it does on this server. Only changed rows are listed — anything not mentioned is untouched.

PatternWhy it keeps showing up
A cooldown appearing where there was noneInstant-cast skills with no cooldown are button-mashing. Everything near-instant gets a short one.
Big area skills getting much longer cooldownsAnd hitting proportionally harder. Landing one becomes a decision rather than a rotation.
Crowd control durations dropping hardNothing on this server locks a player for more than six seconds. Crowd control opens a window; it is not a win button.
Damage formulas losing their HP or SP termDamage never scales off your own health. A tank that also nukes is removed everywhere.
Buffs going to 30 minutesMaintenance buffs stop being busywork. Tactical ones stay short.
Crafting material counts fallingTwenty toad skins per poison was tedium, not cost. The cooldown is the limiter; the material is only a price.

Frontline

Rune Knight — the bruiser

Two weapon identities. Spear is the diver: go in, hit the main target, weave debuffs and Dragon Breath. Sword is the auto-attacker: Giant Growth, Enchant Blade and Two-Hand Quicken drive the damage, with area skills on cooldown.

The tree is trimmed. Rune Knight drops Bash, Pierce, Bowling Bash, Spear Boomerang, Spiral Pierce, Frenzy and the rest of the old Knight damage list. Buffs and passives stay — Endure, Concentration, Aura Blade, Parry, the masteries. A character still levelling Knight keeps everything; the trimming applies once they graduate.

Runes get cheaper. Most recipes lose a monster part: Nauthiz drops Destroyed Armor, Thurisaz drops Claw of Desert Wolf, Hagalaz drops Round Shell, Lux Anima needs two Gold instead of three.

Royal Guard — the wall

Chooses who cannot die, and pays for it. Devotion works the damage out against the protected ally's defence and hands it straight to the Royal Guard — skipping their own armour, Auto Guard and Reflect Shield. Protecting a squishy means eating squishy-sized hits.

Royal Guard sits outside the damage bands on purpose, which is why so many answers exist. Shadow Chaser strips weapon and shield straight through Chemical Protection, and every shield buff ends the moment the shield is gone. Dark Claw stops melee reflect entirely. Reflect Shield only ever reflected melee anyway. And Leech End ignores both reduction and reflect.

Crafting costs fall sharply. Seed of Thorny Plant goes from 10 Prickly Fruit to 3. Bloodsuck Plant Seed from 10 Maneater Root to 3. Bomb Mushroom Spore from 10+2+5 to 3+1+1. Alcohol drops to one Poison Spore and one Stem. And Mandragora Flowerpot, Oil Bottle and the Fire Expansion powders — which normally only drop from monsters — go on the class vendor outright.

Strikers

Asura is the setup, not the kill. A marked target takes 35% from the Asura itself, then a doubled Gate of Hell finishes it while everybody else on your team is also hitting it 25% harder. Inside one 20 second mark you get two Tiger Cannons and two Gates of Hell.

Open world MVPs keep the 70% cap, so a Sura stays a genuine menace to anything roaming a field map. Everybody can be a Sura, so that is not an exclusive advantage.

Dark Claw is untouched and it is the reason the class works — the damage window between EDPs, and it stops the target reflecting melee at all.

The poison line is the anti-tank toolkit. Leech End deals the victim's VIT and a percentage of their max health per second, ignoring defence and reflect entirely. Death Hurt cuts healing by 20%, which is the natural answer to a priest who will not die.

Strip runs on its own diminishing counter — 5s, then 2.5s, then 1.25s, then immune — kept separate from everything else so Rogues keep their edge.

Copied skills automatically inherit our cooldowns. Copied Asura, Tiger Cannon, Gate of Hell and Tetra Vortex are unusable, since they need Fury, a combo or spheres.

Range

Warlock — the artillery

Pokes with cast-time spells, commits big area damage at the right moment, and pops Telekinesis Intense to go all in. Keeps every Mage and Wizard spell.

Single-target spells are untouched. Bolts, Jupitel Thunder, Soul Expansion and Chain Lightning still have no cooldown — the cast time is the limiter, and that is enough.

Mechanic structures are the one exception to the summon budget. Together they reach 80 to 100% of the Mechanic's own output, because they are stationary and the class is built around them. Everybody else's summons cap at 50 to 60%.

Stealth Field and Neutral Barrier were the camping tools — both had longer durations than cooldowns, which meant permanent uptime. Both now have real gaps.

Support

Arch Bishop — the pillar

A healing machine, not a deleter. Spot heals are spammable and limited by SP; percentage heals sit on long cooldowns for the moments that matter. Heal priest and battle priest are separate specs — an Arch Bishop cannot learn everything.

Healers are meant to be the prime target. One Heal restores about 4–5% of a geared character's health. Two Arch Bishops out-heal a single big hit in about a second — so killing through them takes two or three finishers landed inside a one to two second window. That is the intended shape of a fight, and it is why the answers exist: silence, Death Hurt, dispel, and a Guillotine Cross diving the healer.

Minstrel and Wanderer — the performer

Reads the fight and picks the right song or dance; gets it wrong and dies. Screen-wide buffs and debuffs make the performer the first target on the field. Gender-locked — male becomes Minstrel, female becomes Wanderer, and about 90% of the kit is shared.

Both sexes can fill both slots, so the one-song-one-dance rule works either way round. The counters are straightforward: kill the performer, dispel them, or silence them.

What this means for a group

Stacking a class is fine if you spread it, wasteful if you concentrate it.

  • Marks never stack. Two Suras on one target is one wasted Sura. On two objectives, both are working.
  • Performers do not stack. One is the full effect.
  • Area effects thin out when they overlap. Sixteen plants in one doorway are not sixteen plants.
  • Burst overkills. Two executioners on the same target means the second one hit a corpse.

Which means a guild with six Suras is not wrong — they are a guild that has to attack six places at once. That is a strategy, and multiclassing means nobody is stuck with a composition that did not work.
