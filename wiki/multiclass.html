Multiclassing

One character holds every class. You do not roll an alt, you change job.

How it works

Unlock a class once and it stays unlocked. Walk into your faction city, change into it, and carry on with the same character, the same name, and everything you have collected.

  • Swapping happens at an NPC in your faction city. Not mid-fight, not mid-siege. You commit to a role for the thing you are about to do.
  • Everything you have earned follows you. Bestiary milestones, card registrations, and the permanent bonuses from both apply in every class you hold.
  • Human classes only, all the way through 4th job. No Doram, no Baby classes.

What it actually changes

Three things, and they matter more than the convenience.

Nobody is locked out of content

No dungeon needs a particular class to clear, because you can simply be that class. A party that needs a healer stops looking for one and decides who becomes one.

Your character becomes a serious investment

Months of collection progress, every class, all on one entity. That is the point, and it is why alts are pointless here rather than merely discouraged.

Party identity shifts from recruiting to agreeing

A group does not hunt for the missing role, it decides who takes it. Whether that is better depends on what you liked about the old way.

The honest trade-off. Grouping gets much easier. The flip side is that your class stops being your identity — nobody is the priest any more, because anyone can be. We think that trade is worth making. You might not.

Gear on job change

Changing into a class you have not played leaves you holding nothing it can use. Every job change grants a starter set — weapon, offhand, armour, garment, boots and headgear, appropriate to the class and the tier.

These are Trainee items. They cannot be sold, traded or dropped, and they are meant to be replaced quickly.
