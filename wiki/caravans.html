The war

Caravans Sieges

The zeny economy. Cities pay for convoys, escorts and bandits earn from them, and guilds fund their wars on the proceeds.

Who gets what

WhoPaysEarns
The factionCivic contribution from the city treasuryMilitary points for the faction pool
The escortNothing. Their time and their risk.Zeny, personally
The raiderNothing at allZeny, personally
Grinders who never escortNothingTheir contribution is what pays for the convoys
Escorting is the best zeny per hour in the game, at any level. A party of two at level 90 earns roughly what a fully geared endgame farmer makes grinding. This is how a player who is not yet strong enough to farm efficiently makes real money.

Running one

Speak to the caravan quartermaster in any faction city. The city funds the cargo — you pay nothing.

Convoy size is your party size. Three people cannot run a large convoy — the quartermaster will not dispatch one.

Longer routes pay disproportionately. Four legs pays six times one leg, which is the reward for staying exposed.

Consecutive runs must differ. No repeating a route, no two short runs in sequence, and the destination must change.

On the road

  • Convoys never wait for raiders. They leave the moment they are dispatched, at any hour.
  • No fly wings on route maps. Mounts work, and mounts work everywhere else too.
  • The caravan moves slowly and never repairs. Damage taken early is still there at the end.
  • If nobody is hunting, NPC bandits are. Plus a miniboss on the last map before the city.

Raiding

Any citizen can raid. No castle, no guild, no obligation. Queue, get dropped into a convoy already on the road, and go.

RuleDetail
You cannot raid your own factionA hard rule, not a filter
Late cutoffConvoys close to their destination are removed from the queue. Nobody is dropped into a chase they cannot win.
Losing costs nothingNot zeny, not points, not equipment. Only time.

Taking a convoy

The caravan has a control bar. Every raider present drains one second of it per second.

One living defender in the circle halts everything. Not slows it — stops it. A surviving escort does not have to win, they have to stand there and be alive.

Killing raiders speeds the caravan up

Every raider killed while you still hold control adds 10% caravan speed, stacking to 50% for the rest of the leg.

Which means a wipe after killing three is not the same as a wipe after killing none. You pushed the caravan a third further down the road, and the raiders have less distance and less time to work with.

Applies to both sides. A party that keeps throwing itself at a superior opponent spends more of the run dead, and eventually the convoy simply arrives.

What it does to your faction

OutcomeEffect on your city
Delivered105% of the civic outlay comes back. Military points to the faction pool.
LostThe entire outlay is gone, and 60% of it becomes civic for the raiders' faction.

A convoy that arrives costs the city essentially nothing. Losing one costs twenty times what winning returns, and hands most of it to a rival.

Which is why escorting matters. A faction that defends its trade runs it almost for free. A faction that keeps losing convoys is funding its rivals' cities with its own contribution.

A faction cannot commission convoys if it would drop below its city upkeep. Which means a losing faction is forced back to grinding rather than watching its city collapse.
