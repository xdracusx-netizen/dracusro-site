Caravans Sieges

How a run works

A castle-holding guild buys cargo in one city and moves it to another. That cargo is real capital out of the war chest, and delivering it is where almost all war supply comes from.

  • You buy the cargo. Capital at risk, not a free timer.
  • Routes are instanced. Raiders opt in deliberately. Nobody gets dragged into PvP for standing on a road.
  • The convoy is visible to the whole server. Everyone knows a loaded caravan is crossing the desert. The only question is who can get there.
  • Longer hauls pay disproportionately more. A two-leg run beats two single runs, and a round trip beats both.
  • Running the same route repeatedly pays less each time, down to a floor. Variety is enforced by the wallet.

Raiding

Any citizen can raid. No castle, no guild, no siege obligation.

This is the low-stakes entry point to PvP on this server — a small party, a road, and somebody else's money. Raiders are capped per convoy and it is one party at a time, so an interception is a fight rather than a mob.

The road is built for it. Bends, elevation and cover along its length, because a straight corridor means the escort sees you coming from the map edge and the ambush never happens.

Raiding destroys value on purpose

The owner loses everything. The raider recovers only a fraction. Two factions farming each other's convoys burn more than they make. Banditry works against a real enemy and fails as a mutual arrangement.

Morroc is built not to care — it takes a higher cut on interceptions and runs the black market where the stolen goods sell.

Why the cargo has to be bought

If generating a caravan were free, one person with two accounts would run convoys on one and intercept them on the other. With mandatory purchase, the runner spends everything and the raider recovers a fraction — feeding yourself burns capital.

What happens at the end

Successful deliveryThe guild receives war supply on the leg curve. The destination city receives civic contribution.
InterceptionThe raider's faction receives a fraction of the cargo value, plus the physical goods. The owner loses everything.
Successful defenceIf there was a real interception attempt and the escort repelled it, escorts are paid directly.
TimeoutThe caravan is lost. The owner does not recover the capital.

Contraband

A guild that lost its castle keeps trade rights while it holds the right to take it back. Those runs are contraband — hunted by their own faction, because their own faction is currently occupied by somebody else.
